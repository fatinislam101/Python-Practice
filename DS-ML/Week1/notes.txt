10. Your 90-day execution roadmap
The plan below assumes 2–3 hours daily, or roughly 14–21 hours a week. It prioritizes customer validation over feature development.
Days 1–14 · Research and validation
Goal: prove the problem exists
- Research 10 competing products and their actual pricing.
- Build a list of 30 qualified prospects.
- Conduct at least 5–10 meaningful customer conversations.
- Map the current workflow of a potential customer.
- Create a simple mockup or workflow demonstration.
Deliverable: a defined problem, target customer, and initial offer.

Days 15–30 · Demo and first offer
Goal: seek the first payment
- Build the smallest working demonstration.
- Use sample data or customer-authorized test data.
- Create a one-page landing page and a clear service offer.
- Contact qualified prospects consistently.
- Offer a fixed-scope paid pilot.
Deliverable: a demonstrable workflow and at least one serious paid-pilot opportunity.

Days 31–60 · MVP and early customers
Goal: prove the workflow works
- Implement only validated features.
- Add authentication and access controls where needed.
- Test duplicate leads, failed notifications, and missing data.
- Onboard early customers and document their feedback.
- Track actual support time and operating costs.
Deliverable: a reliable small-scale solution with measured usage and costs.

Days 61–90 · Retention and repeatability
Goal: decide whether to productize
- Improve onboarding and remove unnecessary steps.
- Standardize repeated customer configurations.
- Review pricing, retention, and acquisition results.
- Add only integrations requested by multiple customers.
- Decide whether to scale, pivot, or continue as a service.
Deliverable: evidence-based decision on whether the opportunity deserves further investment.

Budget allocation
For the first 30 days, try to keep additional spending below ৳1,500–৳3,000 total if possible—not necessarily per month—by using local development, free tiers, and manual demonstrations. Do not purchase a paid database, premium hosting, and multiple AI subscriptions at the same time.

If a paid pilot requires infrastructure, include those costs in the pilot quote. Never promise unlimited AI or messaging usage without a clear cost model.
11. Final recommendation
My recommendation is to investigate real estate lead recovery first, with lead assignment automation for small agencies as the backup.

Here is the decision in practical terms:
- Customer: A small real estate agency with multiple salespeople and incoming inquiries.
- Urgent problem: Leads are not consistently assigned, contacted, and followed up.
- First version: Capture leads, assign ownership, schedule follow-ups, flag overdue records, and show a simple manager report.
- Reason to pay: The agency needs a measurable improvement in its existing workflow, not another generic AI feature.
- First seven days: Interview prospective customers, map one real workflow, build a demonstration with sample data, and present a paid pilot offer.
- Avoid: A large CRM, AI voice agents, complex WhatsApp integrations, a mobile app, and elaborate dashboards before customers ask for them.
- Initial validation budget: Aim for ৳0–৳3,000 using free development tools and a lightweight demonstration.
- First revenue: Charge for implementation and a short pilot before completing the full SaaS.
The idea is a bad choice if agencies have no meaningful follow-up problem, will not share enough information to test a solution, or already solve the problem satisfactorily with their current tools.

I would change the recommendation if interviews reveal stronger willingness to pay for agency reporting, or if you have significantly easier access to a different group of business owners.

The long-term objective is not merely to own a SaaS. It is to own a repeatable solution to a business problem that customers will continue paying to solve.
